# Market Research: US Backyard Chicken Owners (2026-07-24)

Research compiled to inform go-to-market and service-area prioritization for RoostAssured.

## 1. Prevalence & Demographics

- Estimated **~10-11M US households** keep backyard chickens (~8-13% of households). This is a rough, non-census-grade estimate.
- Best primary source: Elkhoraibi et al. survey (1,487 flock owners, peer-reviewed): owners split roughly evenly across urban/suburban/rural; **41% have household income >$100K**; **44% professional occupations, only 4% in agriculture** — mostly hobbyists, not farmers.
- Motivations: fresh eggs, pet/companionship value, sustainability, food security, and (recently) egg-price inflation hedging.
- **Data gap**: USDA APHIS ran its first national "Backyard Animal Keeping Study" (NAHMS, 2024) with urban-specific arms in Denver and Miami. Results not yet published as of this research — would be the best authoritative source once released.

## 2. Geographic Concentration (proxy-based — no direct density data exists)

No dataset directly measures flock density by metro/county. Converging proxy signals (permissive ordinances, homesteading culture, retail footprint):

- Strongest signal: **Pacific Northwest (Portland, Seattle), Colorado (Denver), Texas (Austin, Dallas-Fort Worth)**, plus pockets of the Southeast (Nashville, Raleigh) and New England.
- Retail proxy: Tractor Supply sells ~11M chicks/year; ~1 in 4 customers has bought chickens; growth concentrated in suburban communities.
- Could not verify specific r/BackYardChickens subscriber counts or city-level Facebook group sizes — treat any such figures found elsewhere as unverified.

## 3. Growth Trends

- **COVID-era surge**: self-sufficiency/home food production driven.
- **2022-2023 spike**: avian flu (58M+ birds affected since 2022) drove record egg prices → sharp rise in chick sales.
- Possible dip below 10M households in 2023, rebound to ~11M in 2024 as shortages persisted (low-confidence, aggregator-sourced).
- **2025**: renewed avian-flu/egg-price pressure reportedly sustained a "coop boom" (per Fortune).

## 4. Regulatory Landscape (illustrative snapshot, not exhaustive — ordinances change often, vary by HOA)

**Permissive:**
- Denver, CO (up to 8 hens, no roosters)
- Arvada, CO (6 hens)
- Aurora, CO (permit, up to 4)
- Commerce City, CO (permitted since 2024)
- Los Angeles, CA (hens + roosters allowed with zoning limits)
- Des Moines / Huxley / Johnston, IA
- Burlington / Bristol / Hinesburg, VT

**Restrictive/variable:**
- Dallas, TX (no roosters)
- Fort Worth, TX (50-ft setback)
- Many HOA-governed suburbs ban poultry regardless of city code

**Trending more permissive:** Owensboro, KY approved a new ordinance in Sept. 2025 — signal of ongoing national liberalization.

## 5. Ranked Target Market Shortlist

1. **Denver-Boulder, CO** — APHIS study site, generous hen limits, high income, strong outdoor/travel culture.
2. **Portland, OR** — Long-standing urban-homesteading capital, affluent, chicken-friendly code.
3. **Seattle metro, WA** — Similar profile; tech-sector income supports paid concierge services.
4. **Austin, TX** — Permissive, high-growth, strong sustainability identity, high disposable income.
5. **Nashville, TN** — Fast-growing exurbs with yards, Southern homesteading culture.
6. **Raleigh-Durham, NC** — Research Triangle affluence plus suburban lot sizes.
7. **Dallas-Fort Worth, TX** — Large population/retail density; core-city restrictions push activity to suburbs (opportunity for county-level targeting).
8. **Minneapolis-St. Paul, MN** — Established permissive ordinances, strong sustainability culture, high incomes.
9. **Burlington/Chittenden County, VT** — Small population but very high per-capita homesteading enthusiasm; good pilot market.
10. **Atlanta suburbs, GA** — Large, wealthy, permissive suburban ring, strong population growth.
11. **Phoenix metro, AZ** — Documented 2023 chick-demand surge, large suburban single-family housing stock.
12. **Miami, FL** — Second APHIS study city, affluent pet-service culture; heat/regulatory patchwork needs local validation.

**Confidence note**: Rankings #1-2 and #4-5 rest on solid regulatory/cultural evidence. The rest are built from indirect proxies (income, ordinance permissiveness, retail footprint) since no dataset directly measures flock density by metro. Revisit once USDA APHIS's NAHMS 2024 study is published for harder numbers.
